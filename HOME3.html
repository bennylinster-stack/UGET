Why now

Demand

Firm baseload is back at the centre of utility planning

Data centre and industrial load growth has put firm baseload back at the centre of utility planning after two decades of treating it as optional.
Licensing

A technology-inclusive NRC pathway now exists

10 CFR Part 53 took effect on 29 April 2026, the first new NRC reactor licensing framework since Part 52 in 1989. It is optional rather than mandatory, sitting alongside Parts 50 and 52, but because it is technology-inclusive by design, developers of non-light-water reactors no longer have to seek exemptions from requirements written for a different technology.
Supply

Enrichment and conversion capacity is constrained

Enrichment and conversion capacity is constrained. A cycle that reduces dependence on enrichment has strategic value it did not have when fuel was cheap and abundant.
Capital

The constraint is reaching an underwriter, not the technology

The constraint on most projects is no longer whether the technology is credible. It is whether a sponsor can reach an institution willing to underwrite it.

The demand nobody is positioned to meet

The advanced nuclear industry is competing for a market that does not yet exist at scale, while the load that would consume it is being built now.

Operating thorium reactor worldwide

China's TMSR-LF1, 2 MW thermal, experimental. It reached criticality in October 2023. Every other programme is pre-criticality.

Molten salt reactors producing commercial power

Anywhere, ever. Sixty years after the technology was first demonstrated, no unit has generated electricity at commercial scale.

Hours the MSRE ran at full power

Oak Ridge National Laboratory, 1965 to 1969. The reactor worked. The programme was ended anyway.

Year the US closed its molten salt programme

Cancelled on budget grounds after funding was withdrawn in 1973. Not for technical failure.

The gap between a technology that worked in 1969 and a market with nothing to buy in 2026 is not a technical gap. It is fifty years of capital going somewhere else.
